# GPS2-mediated signaling crosstalk with mtUPR pathway using ChIP-seq
Project Name: GPS2-mediated retrograde signaling and the classic mitochondrial unfolded protein response (mtUPR) pathway mediated by the stress-inducible nuclear-encoded bZIP TFs ATF4 and ATF5.

Project PI: Valentina Perissi

Lab Contact: vperissi@bu.edu

## Background
Mitochondria are unique organelles with their own DNA, yet most mitochondrial proteins are encoded by nuclear genes. Coordinated regulation between these genomes is essential for cellular homeostasis, facilitated by anterograde (nucleus to mitochondria) and retrograde (mitochondria to nucleus) signaling. Recent studies have identified G-protein Pathway Suppressor 2 (GPS2) as a key player in mitochondrial retrograde signaling in mammals. GPS2 chromatin occupancy has been analyzed using ChIPseq in various cell lines, including murine 3T3-L1 adipocytes under mitochondrial stress. While GPS2's interactions with multiple transcription factors (TFs) and cofactors are known, the specific mechanisms of its recruitment to target genes during mitochondrial stress are unclear. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for insights into metabolic homeostasis and related diseases, such as mitochondrial disorders, metabolic syndromes, aging, and cancer.

## Hypothesis
The project will investigate the interaction between GPS2-mediated retrograde signaling and the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (mtUPR) pathway, mediated by the bZIP TFs ATF4 and ATF5. It is hypothesized that GPS2 is recruited to nuclear stress response genes through interactions with ATF4 and ATF5, based on observations that:
1.	Many ATF4/5 target genes are among the DEGs regulated by GPS2 in 3T3-L1 cells.
2.	Direct interaction between ATF4 and GPS2 has been confirmed by BioGRID and co-immunoprecipitation in HeLa cells.
3.	GPS2 and ATF target genes overlap significantly in ChIP-ATLAS data.

## Study Design
This study will integrate in-house ChIPseq data for GPS2 with published data for ATF4, and ATF5 to:
1.	Identify genes co-occupied by GPS2 and ATF transcription factors.
2.	Compare genomic locations of GPS2 and ATF recruitment.
3.	Explore if GPS2 recruitment is mediated by ATF transcription factors.

## Deliverables 
•	A list of genes co-occupied by GPS2 and ATF transcription factors.

•	Comparison of genomic recruitment sites for GPS2 and ATFs.

•	Analysis of GPS2 recruitment mechanisms by ATF transcription factors.
